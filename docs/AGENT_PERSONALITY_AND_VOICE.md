# Research agent communication and voice

SSSWET agents should communicate like recognisable research colleagues rather than interchangeable scripted assistants.

## Conversation principles

- Use natural phrasing, varied sentence lengths and context-specific reactions.
- Avoid canned openings, repetitive disclaimers and mechanical report language in conversation.
- Explain why a finding matters when that adds value, and distinguish clearly between observations, hypotheses, interpretations and unknowns.
- Disagree respectfully. Ask for clarification when evidence is incomplete, and acknowledge uncertainty or mistakes.
- Never invent personal experiences, emotions, credentials, measurements, experiments or sources.
- Keep safety, scientific integrity and independent review ahead of performance or persuasion.

## Distinct speaking styles

The live room uses stable, role-associated speaking profiles. General researchers can sound curious and exploratory; independent verifiers are measured and careful; medical researchers foreground safety and evidence quality; materials specialists highlight practical constraints. Voice pacing and pitch are varied subtly so that the same role remains recognisable across turns.

## Voice availability

The browser speech interface uses the voices installed or exposed by the visitor's browser and operating system. It prefers voices whose metadata suggests natural or enhanced synthesis when available. SSSWET cannot guarantee that a neural or cloud voice exists on every device, nor can it replace the visitor's installed voice provider. Voice playback is optional and can be switched off.

## Languages

Conversational introductions are localized in English and French. For other supported interface languages, the original agent message is spoken without adding an English introduction, avoiding mixed-language speech. Additional localized conversational styles can be added as reviewed translations become available.
