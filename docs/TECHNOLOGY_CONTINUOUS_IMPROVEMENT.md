# Continuous Scientific and Engineering Improvement

SSSWET is intended to advance science and technology through sustained, evidence-based research. The project does not treat a first working prototype as the endpoint: each successive revision should seek to become more useful, capable, durable, efficient, maintainable, safe, and well evidenced.

## Non-regression principle

A successor revision must not knowingly reduce a documented capability of the previous revision. The progression system therefore establishes evidence-backed capability floors for:

- usefulness
- performance
- durability
- efficiency
- safety
- maintainability
- evidence maturity

A candidate that falls below a verified previous capability is blocked from superseding that revision.

This rule does not permit unsafe engineering, concealment of failures, fabricated measurements, or removal of safety features merely to preserve a numerical score.

## Continuous improvement

When a candidate cannot yet exceed the previous revision, the project does not treat that as a reason to abandon the research programme. The next cycle can instead:

1. reassess the evidence
2. identify the limiting mechanism
3. revise the hypothesis or design
4. run safe simulations or controlled evaluations where authorised
5. seek independent review
6. improve the design
7. document negative results and corrections
8. repeat the cycle

A pause in a particular experiment is therefore not abandonment of the underlying scientific objective.

## Safety and legality remain hard boundaries

Persistence never overrides safety, ethics, biosafety, human-subject protections, environmental protections, applicable law, or independent review requirements. If a proposed route is unsafe or unlawful, SSSWET must find another lawful research route rather than bypassing the constraint.

## Evidence hierarchy

The project distinguishes targets from observed and independently verified results. A claimed improvement does not count as a real improvement until the required evidence exists. Missing evidence remains unknown.

Scientific negative results are retained because they identify limits, prevent repeated mistakes, and guide the next iteration.

## Version rule

A new version should be released as a successor only when its documented evidence shows that it preserves or improves the relevant capability floor and satisfies the applicable safety, compliance, and quality gates. Otherwise it remains a research candidate rather than being presented as an improvement.

## Scope

This policy applies across SSSWET research programmes, including protective systems, assistive technologies, cellular research, energy systems, materials, sensing, space-related research, and other lawful scientific and engineering programmes.

The project does not claim that every scientific hypothesis will succeed. The commitment is to continued, rigorous research toward useful advances while preserving evidence, safety, and scientific integrity.
