# Protective Systems and Exoskeleton Research Portfolio

**Status:** Concept portfolio for feasibility research. None of these models is a tested prototype, certified product or operational military system.

SSSWET may investigate protective and assistive equipment for military, civil-protection, emergency-response and occupational users. The scope is non-offensive: protection, mobility assistance, environmental resilience, injury prevention, evacuation and medical support. Weapons, weapon mounts, targeting, offensive autonomy and combat optimization are excluded.

## Design principles
1. Protection before performance: prioritize injury prevention, safe mobility, situational awareness and emergency removal.
2. Evidence-led ratings: no ballistic, blast, chemical, thermal or radiation rating without independent testing against a named standard and exact configuration.
3. Human factors: assess mass, heat burden, fatigue, hearing, vision, dexterity, fit and long-duration comfort.
4. Fail-safe engineering: manual release, safe power-loss behavior, clear warnings, emergency stop and maintainable components.
5. Lifecycle accountability: disclose materials, manufacturing, testing, repair, replacement, training, maintenance, warranties and end-of-life costs.
6. No price by prestige: premium variants require documented cost and performance evidence.

## Concept models

### Aegis Modular — modular protective armor
**Purpose:** Configurable protective carrier and modular personal-protection platform for defined occupational hazards.

Research directions: replaceable traceable modules; ergonomic load distribution; adjustable fit; hearing, eye and joint protection options; rapid removal; compatibility with medical evacuation equipment.

Variants: **Base** (lightweight and maintainable); **Pro** (higher-durability materials and expanded testing); **Extreme** (specialist harsh-environment configuration, subject to weight, heat and mobility trade-offs).

Validation: independent tests for each hazard and configuration. No protection level is assumed.

### Bastion Impact — impact and environmental protection
**Purpose:** Reduce selected blunt-impact, debris, weather and occupational exposure risks.

Research directions: energy-absorbing liners; replaceable impact components; head, eye, hearing and joint protection; heat and moisture management; compatibility with rescue equipment.

Variants: **Field** (general impact and weather); **Industrial** (high-durability occupational use); **Extreme** (specialized environment with documented limits).

Validation: standardized impact and environmental tests, human-factors trials and published limitations.

### Atlas Assist — powered load-assist exoskeleton
**Purpose:** Reduce physical strain during lifting, carrying and prolonged occupational tasks.

Research directions: posture and load-transfer assistance; safe disengagement; manual override; predictable power-loss behavior; fit, balance, fatigue and musculoskeletal risk studies.

Variants: **Passive** (unpowered support); **Active** (powered assistance for defined tasks); **Pro** (enhanced sensing and diagnostics, only after safety validation).

Validation: representative-user testing, task-specific ergonomics, battery safety and independent failure-mode assessment.

### Lifeline Rescue — rescue and evacuation exoskeleton
**Purpose:** Support safe mobility and equipment handling during rescue, evacuation and disaster response.

Research directions: stable movement assistance; manual recovery; emergency stop and rapid removal; compatibility with stretchers and rescue equipment; operation in rain, dust and uneven environments.

Variants: **Compact** (mobility and light equipment assistance); **Heavy** (higher load-assistance capacity subject to ergonomic validation); **Pro** (advanced diagnostics and environmental hardening).

Validation: controlled rescue-task assessments; no autonomous force against people.

### Sentinel Environment — protective environmental suit
**Purpose:** Protect users from specified environmental exposures.

Research directions: thermal management; certified particulate and chemical protection components; breathability and moisture control; emergency doffing; physiological monitoring with clear limitations.

Variants: **Climate** (cold, heat and weather); **Containment** (certified components for defined contamination settings); **Extreme** (specialist multi-hazard configuration only where compatible standards and tests exist).

Validation: hazard-specific certification, exposure testing, human-factors assessment and clear operating limits.

### Guardian Monitor — non-diagnostic safety monitoring
**Purpose:** Provide alerts about environmental exposure and selected physiological strain indicators.

Research directions: heat-strain and fatigue indicators; environmental sensors; prioritized alerts; data minimization; privacy and cybersecurity.

Validation: sensor accuracy, false-alarm analysis, usability and privacy review. It does not replace clinical assessment.

## Cost and procurement framework
Each model must be costed through documented low, base and high scenarios. Inputs include materials and component traceability; engineering and specialist review hours; prototype fabrication and independent testing; batteries, sensors, software maintenance and replacement parts; training, fitting, inspection, repair and warranty; applicable taxes, shipping and lifecycle support.

A high-end version may cost substantially more when verified materials, testing, reliability and support justify it. A premium label alone is not evidence of better protection. Missing cost inputs remain unknown; no price is invented.

## Responsible-use boundaries
- No weapons or weapon attachments are designed, supplied or integrated.
- No targeting, offensive autonomy or combat-effectiveness optimization.
- No claim of military-grade, bulletproof, blast-proof, chemical-proof or radiation-proof performance without independent certification for the exact configuration.
- Procurement requires legal review, export-control assessment where applicable, human-rights and end-use due diligence, privacy and cybersecurity review.
- Field evaluation requires informed participation, occupational safety controls and independent oversight.

## Current maturity
All six models are conceptual research directions. They have no assigned certification, verified performance rating, delivery date or official price. Advancement requires evidence, prototype safety review, independent testing and documented lifecycle costing.
