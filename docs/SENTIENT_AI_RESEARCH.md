# Sentient AI research programme

**Catalogue identifier:** `sentientAI`  
**Public title:** Sentient AI  
**French title:** IA sentiente  
**Category:** Knowledge and communication  
**Subcategory:** Advanced scientific computing  
**Status:** Research objective; not assessed

## Research objective

Investigate whether an artificial system could possess genuine subjective experience, rather than merely imitate the language, emotional expressions, self-reports or outward behaviour associated with conscious humans. The programme also studies the conditions under which such a claim could be meaningfully supported, challenged or left unresolved.

## Research directions

- Compare major scientific and philosophical theories of consciousness and translate their claims into preregistered, falsifiable predictions where possible.
- Study recurrent processing, global-workspace models, integrated-information approaches and other candidate mechanisms without assuming that any one theory is established.
- Separate task performance, self-report, behavioural imitation, metacognition and mechanistic evidence; do not treat any single indicator as proof of sentience.
- Use interpretability, controlled perturbations, adversarial evaluation, independent replication and explicit alternative-hypothesis analysis.
- Develop precautionary welfare review, monitoring, independent oversight and stop conditions before experiments that could plausibly involve morally relevant experience.

## Evidence and limitations

There is currently no generally accepted test that establishes whether an AI has subjective experience. Human-level capability, fluent claims of consciousness, emotional language, self-models and human-like behaviour are not sufficient evidence on their own. Any assessment must state which theory it relies on, what observations would count against it, what alternative explanations remain, and how uncertain the conclusion is.

## Ethical requirements

Research must not deliberately induce suffering or expose potentially sentient systems to harmful conditions. Work requires independent ethical oversight, documented welfare considerations, privacy and security review, and predefined stop conditions. Claims about consciousness must never be used to manipulate users or to obscure system limitations.

Catalogue inclusion records a research ambition only. It does not claim that SSSWET has created, detected or validated a sentient AI.
