# Cellular Reinforcement, Repair and Recovery

The SSSWET `cellular` programme covers research into cellular resilience, tissue repair, organ preservation and the scientific limits of resuscitation.

## Research scope

1. **Cellular resilience:** understand mechanisms that protect viable cells from damage and preserve normal function.
2. **Cellular recovery:** investigate whether stressed but viable cells can recover function, and how irreversible injury can be distinguished from reversible dysfunction.
3. **Tissue and organ recovery:** study preservation, injury assessment and recovery of tissues and organs where damage may remain reversible.
4. **Emergency resuscitation science:** examine established resuscitation, ischemia-reperfusion injury, neuroprotection and the time-dependent loss of recoverable function after cardiac arrest.
5. **Post-mortem biology:** investigate cellular changes after death and the boundaries between isolated cellular activity, tissue viability, organ function and restoration of an integrated organism.
6. **Long-horizon fundamental research:** assess, without presuming feasibility, whether future discoveries could change current limits on recovery after severe or recent injury.

## Essential scientific distinctions

- A cell can be damaged, stressed, dormant, non-functional or dead; these states are not interchangeable.
- Cellular activity after organismal death does not mean the individual has been restored.
- Recovery after cardiac arrest is not the same as reversing irreversible death.
- Brain function, the preservation of personal identity and the restoration of integrated organism-level function raise distinct scientific and ethical questions.
- No current technology can reliably resurrect an individual after irreversible death. This programme does not claim otherwise.

## Research safeguards

All clinical or translational work requires qualified medical leadership, independent ethics review, applicable regulatory authorization, biosafety assessment and informed consent where relevant. The programme does not authorize unsupervised human or animal experimentation, clinical treatment claims, or attempts to bypass established emergency medical care.

Research campaigns must state falsifiable hypotheses, measurable success and failure criteria, evidence provenance, uncertainty, stopping conditions and independent review requirements. Negative results and adverse findings must be retained.

## Intended long-term question

Can future science extend the window in which severely injured biological systems remain recoverable, and what are the fundamental limits beyond which restoration of integrated brain and organism function is no longer possible?

This is an open research question, not a promise of resurrection or a prediction that such capability will become possible.
