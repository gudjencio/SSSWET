# Cellular Research Campaign Proposals

**Status:** Draft proposals. These campaigns are not activated, funded, experimentally executed or clinically authorized.

## 1. Cellular Recovery and Resilience
**Objective:** Map mechanisms associated with cellular stress, reversible dysfunction, repair and irreversible injury.

**Working hypothesis:** Some cellular dysfunction is reversible within context-dependent limits; validated indicators may help distinguish recovery potential from irreversible damage.

**Success criteria:** A source-linked taxonomy; independent review of candidate indicators; reproducible synthesis of published evidence, including negative findings.

**Failure criteria:** Indicators fail replication, cannot distinguish relevant states, or evidence is too heterogeneous to support conclusions.

**Roles:** General research, medical review, data analysis and independent verification.

**Safety gates:** Literature-first. No unsupervised biological experimentation. Any laboratory proposal requires qualified institutional review and biosafety authorization.

## 2. Tissue and Organ Preservation and Recovery
**Objective:** Assess evidence for preserving and recovering tissues or organs after potentially reversible injury.

**Working hypothesis:** Organ-specific preservation strategies may extend recoverable function in defined settings, but cannot be generalized to whole-person recovery.

**Success criteria:** Evidence map separating cell, tissue, organ and organism outcomes; defined outcome measures; independent review of translational feasibility and safety.

**Failure criteria:** Apparent recovery is limited to surrogate markers, fails replication, or risks outweigh plausible benefit.

**Roles:** Medical research, materials science, data analysis and independent verification.

**Safety gates:** No human or animal intervention without qualified leadership, ethics approval, regulatory authorization, informed consent where applicable and appropriate facilities.

## 3. Neural Preservation and Reversible Dysfunction
**Objective:** Examine evidence concerning brain injury, neuroprotection, preservation of function and the distinction between reversible dysfunction and irreversible loss.

**Working hypothesis:** Better characterization of injury progression may improve understanding of recoverable neurological function. No assumption is made that personal identity or consciousness can be restored.

**Success criteria:** A reviewed framework distinguishing cellular activity, network function, consciousness and person-level outcomes; independent evidence-quality review.

**Failure criteria:** Proposed measures conflate isolated activity with meaningful neurological function, or evidence cannot support safe interpretation.

**Roles:** Medical research, general research, data analysis, ethics review and independent verification.

**Safety gates:** Strict clinical, ethics and regulatory oversight; no experiments on people, no treatment advice and no delay of emergency care.

## 4. Post-Mortem Biological Limits
**Objective:** Review post-mortem cellular and tissue changes and define the limits of current evidence.

**Working hypothesis:** Different cell types and tissues may retain biological activity for different periods after organismal death, but such activity does not establish restoration of an integrated organism or person.

**Success criteria:** Source-linked map of observations; explicit distinction between cell activity, tissue viability, organ function and organism-level recovery; independent review.

**Failure criteria:** Evidence does not support extrapolation beyond the observed biological level, or organismal restoration claims cannot be substantiated.

**Roles:** General research, medical research, data analysis, ethics review and independent verification.

**Safety gates:** Fundamental and literature-based research until qualified institutions establish lawful, ethical and safe scope. No attempts to revive or experiment on deceased persons.

## Shared governance
- All proposals remain drafts until source review, risk assessment and qualified human approval.
- No fabricated sources, agent endorsements, experimental results or budgets.
- Simulation, published observation, independent replication and clinical evidence remain distinct.
- Negative results and corrections are retained.
- No current technology reliably reverses irreversible death or guarantees resurrection.
