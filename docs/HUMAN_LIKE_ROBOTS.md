# Human-like Robots research programme

**Catalogue identifier:** humanLikeRobots  
**Public title:** Human-like Robots  
**French title:** Robots humanoïdes  
**Category:** Robotics and autonomous systems  
**Subcategory:** Assistive robotics and mobility  
**Status:** Research objective; not assessed

## Research objective

Explore human-shaped or human-inspired robots that help people in everyday life and can become trusted companions through respectful, transparent and consensual interaction. Potential applications include household assistance, accessibility, support for older adults and people with disabilities, education, social interaction and collaborative work.

The programme may study integration with advanced conversational AI and, as a separate research question, systems investigated by the Sentient AI programme. A humanoid body, convincing conversation or apparent friendship must never be treated as evidence that a robot is conscious or has feelings.

## Research directions

- Human-centred mechanical design, expressive but non-deceptive interfaces, accessible controls and maintainable components.
- Safe navigation and manipulation around people, children, pets and wildlife, including collision avoidance, compliant movement, personal-space awareness and conservative force and speed limits.
- Everyday assistance that remains under the user's control, with clear consent before actions, physical contact, recording or access to private spaces.
- Social interaction that supports companionship without encouraging emotional dependency, impersonation, coercion or deceptive claims about the robot's identity.
- Privacy-preserving perception, on-device processing where feasible, data minimization, secure updates, access controls and user-controlled deletion.
- Reliability engineering, emergency stops, safe shutdown, fault detection, recovery procedures and independent testing in representative environments.
- Accessibility, cultural and language adaptation, affordability, repairability, environmental impact and long-term social evaluation.

## Non-harm and safety requirements

- No robot may be designed, trained, configured or marketed to injure or kill a human or an animal.
- Weaponization, attack behaviours and deliberate physical aggression are outside the programme.
- Default physical behaviour must be protective and non-confrontational: maintain safe distance, avoid collisions, stop when uncertain, yield to people and animals, and request human assistance when needed.
- Physical interaction requires validated force, speed, reach and workspace limits, accessible emergency stops, fail-safe states and documented risk assessment.
- The system must not pursue, corner, intimidate, restrain or intentionally frighten people or animals.
- Human oversight, clear operating boundaries and an effective way for users to pause or disable the robot are mandatory.

## Transparency and ethical requirements

Robots must clearly disclose that they are artificial systems. Their design must not falsely claim human identity, genuine emotions or consciousness. Companionship features should respect user autonomy and avoid manipulation, coercion or exploitative dependency. Users must be able to understand what the robot senses, when it records information, what data it retains and how to delete it.

Human-like appearance and social behaviour do not establish sentience. Any possible subjective experience belongs to the separate Sentient AI research question and requires its own evidence standards and precautionary welfare review.

## Evidence and limitations

Catalogue inclusion records a research ambition only. It does not establish that a generally capable, safe, conscious or commercially available humanoid robot exists. Every prototype requires qualified engineering review, applicable legal and privacy assessment, independent safety testing and transparent reporting of failures and limitations.
